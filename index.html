Nearby signals

    Signals show up here a few seconds after a scan starts. A typical home has 5 to 40.

    How to sweep

    1. Start near the middle of the home and give the list 10 seconds to fill.
    2. Walk slowly toward each room and pause 2–3 seconds at each spot. The reading is a 2-second average, so it lags a step behind you.
    3. Tap the signal that climbs as you get closer. In the same room a phone usually reads −75 dBm or stronger; within a metre, about −55.
    4. To get a direction, hold this phone flat against your chest and turn slowly. Your body blocks 10 dB or more, so the lost phone is behind you where the reading is weakest.
    5. Cushions, bags and coat pockets cost 5–15 dB. A strong reading with nothing in sight means look inside things.

    If nothing climbs

    • A dead battery or Bluetooth switched off leaves nothing to hear. Find Hub still shows where the phone last checked in.
    • Some phones send few Bluetooth signals while idle. Stay longer at each spot, or ring it from Find Hub.
    • Phones change their Bluetooth address about every 15 minutes. The finder follows the change when the new signal looks the same. If the signal drops out, pick it again in the list.
    • If this page won't run, the free nRF Connect app from the Play Store lists the same signals with live dBm.
